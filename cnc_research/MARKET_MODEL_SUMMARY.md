## Competitive Landscape

**Direct Competitors:**
- **G-Wizard Calculator** (CNCCookbook) - Subscription + free tier with horsepower limits; $50-150/yr estimated
- **HSMAdvisor** - $65 one-time purchase; no recurring revenue
- **FSWizard** - Free web app + mobile; no monetization visible
- **Kennametal, Sandvik, etc.** - Free web calculators (vendor lock-in strategy)

**Competitive Threat:** VERY HIGH
- Free alternatives cover 80% of use cases
- Differentiation must come from: (1) superior accuracy/database, (2) CAM integrations, (3) collaboration features, (4) specific material/machine support
- Price sensitivity in market is high (many users won't pay $200/yr if free option exists)

## Critical Validation Gaps

**High Priority (Must Validate Before Launch):**
1. **Willingness to pay:** Survey 100+ machinists - Is $150-250/yr realistic, or should price be $49-99?
2. **Adoption rate:** Interview 20+ medium shops - What % would actually switch from spreadsheets/free tools?
3. **Feature gaps:** What do G-Wizard, HSMAdvisor, spreadsheets NOT do well? (This is your moat)
4. **CAM integration potential:** Are CAM vendors (Mastercam, Fusion 360, SolidCAM) open to partnerships?

**Medium Priority:**
1. Segment size validation - Are there really 4,000 medium shops with 5-50 machines?
2. Churn benchmarking - What's realistic churn for this category? (Assumed 15%, could be 5-30%)
3. CAC payback validation - Can we acquire users at $150-250 CAC with reasonable payback period?

## Go-to-Market Strategy (Recommended)

**Phase 1: Validate & Build (Months 1-6)**
- Launch with freemium model: Free tier (hobbyists, evaluators), Pro tier ($9.99/month or $99/year)
- Survey 100+ machinists on pricing, features, pain points
- Interview 10 medium shops for early access program
- Build CAM software integration (start with Fusion 360, most accessible)

**Phase 2: Market to Medium Shops (Months 6-18)**
- Target small/medium shops (5-50 machines) - highest willingness to pay, easiest distribution
- CAM vendor partnerships/co-sells (e.g., Mastercam bundle deals)
- Content marketing: Blog, YouTube, technical forums (CNCZone, PracticalMachinist)
- Industry trade show presence (IMTS, Eastec)

**Phase 3: Scale to Large Shops (Year 2+)**
- Direct sales team for enterprises (50+ machines)
- Enterprise features: Team licensing, SSO, API access, analytics
- Position as "CAD/CAM extension" not "point calculator tool"

## Realistic Expectations & Risks

**Upside Potential:**
- Medium shops represent $6.2M addressable market; if you capture 20-30%, that's $1.2-1.8M ARR by year 3
- Large shops represent $16.8M; capturing 10% = $1.6M; not realistic until year 3+ but high leverage
- Total TAM of $24M suggests acquisition potential: If you reach $5M ARR (EBITDA positive), could be acquisition target for Autodesk, Siemens, Dassault at 5-8x revenue = $25-40M deal

**Downside Risks:**
1. **Pricing power challenge:** If actual willingness-to-pay is $50/yr (not $150-250/yr), TAM shrinks 60%+ and profitability is marginal
2. **Free tool competition:** G-Wizard could bundle "advanced feeds/speeds" into free tier; FSWizard could monetize; new entrants (ChatGPT-based calculator?) could emerge
3. **Spreadsheet stickiness:** Excel is ubiquitous and has zero switching cost; overcoming this requires 3-5x productivity gains (hard to prove)
4. **CAM integration complexity:** Major CAM vendors may not prioritize third-party integrations; this limits differentiation
5. **Distribution challenge:** Most adoption is through forums/word-of-mouth; paid acquisition may cost more than $150-250 ARPU, making unit economics unfavorable

**Why This Is Still Viable:**
- CAM market is $74K licenses × $500-2K per seat = $37-148M in the US; feeds/speeds calculator is 0.2-2% of that
- Professional machinists spend $1K-3.5K/yr on software; $200/yr for calculators is only 6-20% of that budget
- Network effects: If 30% of shops in a region use your tool, adoption becomes self-reinforcing (peer recommendations, shared standards)
- Consolidation opportunity: This category could be rolled into CAD/CAM software suites; not a standalone business long-term
